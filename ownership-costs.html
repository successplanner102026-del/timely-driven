OWNERSHIP · BUDGETING

The real cost of owning a car

A realistic car budget includes the purchase price—and the regular costs that arrive after you bring it home.

Start with the one-time costs

  • Down payment or purchase price
  • Sales taxes, registration, title, and dealer fees where applicable
  • Pre-purchase inspection and immediate repairs
  • Winter tires or other equipment required for your climate and local rules

Then calculate recurring costs

  • Loan or lease payment
  • Fuel or electricity
  • Insurance premiums
  • Scheduled maintenance, repairs, and replacement tires
  • Parking, tolls, permits, and roadside assistance

Leave room for surprises

Repairs are irregular, so a monthly budget should set aside money for maintenance and unexpected problems. Costs vary by vehicle age, model, driving habits, location, and condition.

Compare vehicles on the same basis

When comparing two cars, estimate costs over the same period and for a similar amount of driving. A lower purchase price may not mean lower total ownership cost if the vehicle needs more maintenance, consumes more fuel, or is more expensive to insure.

Ask about insurance before buying

Request an insurance quote for the exact vehicle and intended drivers before you finalize a purchase. Coverage availability and price can depend on several factors, and a general online estimate is not a guarantee.
